# Yamaha grand pianos: research notes

Web research, 19 September 2026, for the Signature Pianos Yamaha buyer's guide. Main source: Yamaha Japan's list
of domestic grand models with production dates and prices [1]. Dimensions from Yamaha Japan/USA/Australia spec
pages where published; pre-1994 dimensions from Japanese dealer listings.

Whole-section cautions:
- Suffix letters were reused: G3E = 1971–85 and 1990–94; C3B = 1971–79 and 1988–90; C3A = 1985–88 and 2007–08;
  a plain "C3" was sold in four periods. Date a grand by its serial, not its suffix.
- 1970s G3/C3/C3B are 1830 mm; post-1979 C3 is 1860 mm.
- Many 1970s–mid-80s Japanese-market grands have only 2 pedals (no sostenuto), including a 1980 C3.

## 5. Silent and player naming

S / SN / SG (older), SH from 2013, SH2 from 2018, SH3 from 2022 (e.g. C3X-SH3). D prefix = Disklavier. PE polished
ebony, FP French Provincial, CP/Ch Chippendale, TA2 TransAcoustic [1,12,14,15].

## General notes

- Length is measured from the key slip to the tail; longer grands have bigger soundboards and longer strings [16].
  Japanese size classes: 1 ≈ 1610, 2 ≈ 1690–1730, 3 ≈ 1830–1860, 5 ≈ 1970–2000, 7 ≈ 2230–2270 mm.
- Yamaha room rule: total wall length at least 10 × the piano's length, plus about 600 mm behind the keys for the
  bench [16]. GB1K 15.1 m (≈3.8 m square room); C1 16.1 m (≈4.0 m); C2 17.3 m (≈4.3 m); C3 18.6 m (≈4.65 m);
  C5 20 m (≈5 m); C7 22.7 m (≈5.7 m).
- Place away from direct sun, air-con vents, bathroom humidity and above in-floor heating [16,18].
- Weights 261 kg (GB1K) to 415 kg (C7X). AS/NZS 1170.1 residential floors: 1.5 kPa spread, 1.8 kN point load [34].
  Check upper floors and older suspended timber floors with a builder or engineer [18].
- Legs and pedal lyre come off; the body travels on its side on a piano board [18].
- Serial on the frame, treble side, visible with the lid open [18].
- CX: 3 prop positions plus stopper; GB1K, GC, C-TD: 2 [4,8,10,12]. Prop stopper new on the 1999 L series [3].
- Watch for 2-pedal pianos (1970s–mid-80s G series, some c.1980 C3s, Z1B) and bass-sustain middle pedals (AU/US
  GB1K; US GC1 before 2009). Many Japanese-market grands have a hand-operated practice muffler [22,25].
- Yamaha's full overhaul: new strings and tuning pins, understring felts, agraffes polished or replaced, new
  hammers with rebalanced key leads, damper felts, key bushing cloth, pedal bushings [19]. Drying can crack
  soundboards and loosen pins; fix is oversize pins, usually with restringing [17].
- Japan's fair-trade rules: 中古品 "used" can be sold near as-is; 再調整品 "reconditioned" means inspected,
  cleaned, parts replaced and regulated; "Renewal" is a Yamaha trademark for Yamaha Piano Service rebuilds [20,21].
- Yamaha seasons pianos for their destination and gives no factory warranty outside the country of sale [17].
  UK importer of 3,800+ Japanese Yamahas reports no humidity failures (commercial interest) [31].
- Grand serials (Yamaha USA, Japanese production, grand column) [14]: 1972 1,358,500; 1975 1,935,000; 1980
  3,040,000; 1985 4,040,700; 1988 4,561,000; 1990 4,810,900; 1994 5,291,500; 2000 5,860,000; 2007 6,191,000;
  2012 6,340,000; 2021 6,520,000. Treat as ±1 year.
