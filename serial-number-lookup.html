Finding the number

Both makers stamp the serial number into the gold-coloured iron frame inside the piano, usually beside the model name. If a tuner has left a tuning card inside, it may be written there too.

Good questions

How exact is the year?

Close, but not to the month. The charts give the first serial number of each year, and both makers say the real dates can vary either side, partly because different models were numbered in batches. Treat the answer as the year your piano was made, give or take a few months.

A piano can also sit in a warehouse or showroom for a while, so the year it was first sold may be a year or two later.

My serial number has a letter in it

On a Yamaha, a letter joined to the front of the number names the factory: J for Jakarta in Indonesia, H for Hangzhou in China, T or U for the United States and YT for Taiwan. A letter stamped with a gap before the number is usually part of the model name, and the piano was made in Japan.

On a Kawai, F means Indonesia and A means the United States. Kawai says to ignore any other letter at the start.

Type the number as you see it, gap and all, and the lookup will read it the right way.

Can the number tell me the model?

Not on its own. The model name, such as U1 or C3 on a Yamaha or K-300 on a Kawai, is usually stamped or printed beside the serial number.

Does the age of a piano matter?

Less than you might think. How a piano was looked after matters more than when it was made: a well-kept Japanese piano from the 1980s can play better than a neglected one from 2010.

Age tells a technician where to look, because hammers, strings and tuning pins wear with playing and with the weather. That’s why every piano we sell is checked in our Melbourne workshop and comes with a 10-year warranty.

What if my piano isn’t a Yamaha or Kawai?

Most makers kept serial records. The Pierce Piano Atlas collects them for thousands of brands, and a piano technician can usually date a piano from its number and case.
